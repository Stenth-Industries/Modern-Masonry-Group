How this works

Three engagements.
One system.

STENTH retainers are priced against the system being built — not the hours being spent.

Three tiers exist because three distinct growth problems exist. Foundation solves credibility — operators whose work outpaces their presence. Growth solves predictability — businesses tired of referral luck. Scale solves compounding — operators ready to engineer market expansion rather than chase it.

Each tier is a complete system at that level of ambition. You do not buy deliverables from STENTH. You buy the machine that produces a specific economic outcome, designed and staffed to match where the business actually is.

The questions that determine tier fit are not about budget. They are diagnostic: Where does revenue come from today? Where should it come from in eighteen months? What has to exist between those two points that does not exist now? The answer points to one tier. Pricing follows.

STENTH Custom engagements
Custom engagements

How a custom engagement
gets scoped.

Most agencies scope a retainer by asking what you want. STENTH scopes by diagnosing what is actually blocking you — then designs the engagement backward from the constraint. Price follows the system. The system does not follow the price.

01

Diagnostic intake · 1–2 weeks

Five lenses: revenue composition, funnel economics, positioning audit, demand surface, conversion mechanics. The output is a single artifact — a ranked growth constraint map. Not a list of problems. A diagnosis of what, specifically, is keeping the business from its next step-change.

02

Hypothesis & opportunity sizing

From the constraint map, we isolate the 2–3 levers that unlock the most compounding growth — then quantify the revenue delta between current and solved trajectory. That number becomes the engagement's economic frame. Most agencies skip this step because they can't quantify their own impact. We skip engagements where we can't.

03

System design

Architecture, not a deliverables list. What needs to exist that doesn't today. What needs to be retired. What needs to be rebuilt. And critically — sequencing. What must be fixed before anything else can compound.

04

Resource & cadence mapping

Which specialists, at what cadence, for how long. Weekly sprints, monthly strategic reviews, quarterly positioning resets. This is also where we define what we need from you — the decisions, access, and buy-in without which the engagement stalls. Most retainers die here and no one names it.

05

Engagement design

The retainer document gets written last. Scope boundaries — explicitly what's in, explicitly what's not. Leading indicators weekly, lagging indicators quarterly. Review cadence for proving progress or admitting a hypothesis was wrong. And exit criteria — when the custom engagement graduates into a lighter retainer, or ends.

The mechanical point

Every phase above exists to answer one question: is a long-term retainer actually the right move for this business, or is something simpler the better call? Selling a custom engagement to a business that doesn't need one is how agencies make short-term revenue and long-term enemies.
